FAQ

Common questions.

Audible files use DRM (digital rights management) which prevents any app — including Atlas — from converting them directly. If you own the audiobook and have a DRM-free copy (such as an MP3 version from a different source), Atlas works perfectly. Atlas does not remove DRM and does not assist with removing DRM.
Atlas re-encodes audio to AAC (the M4B container format) using Apple's AVFoundation engine. For most source files, this is a fast, high-quality transcode. The output defaults to 64kbps mono (ideal for spoken word) with an optional 128kbps stereo setting for music or high-quality narration.
Audio-to-M4B conversion is fast — typically 5–15 minutes per audiobook, depending on length and your Mac's speed. ePub/PDF/MOBI conversion via TTS takes longer: expect roughly 1 hour for a full-length novel (roughly 300 pages). You can start a batch and let it run overnight.
Yes. Atlas uses the Nero chapter format (chpl atoms), which is what Apple Books uses for chapter navigation. Chapters are tested on iPhone, iPad, Mac, and CarPlay. If Apple Books shows a chapter list for a file, Atlas-produced files will show one too.
The base app ($14.99) converts audio files — MP3s, ripped CDs, downloaded audiobooks — into properly formatted M4B files with chapters and metadata. AAC+ ($9.99 upgrade) adds the ability to import ePub, PDF, and MOBI ebook files and convert them to M4B audiobooks using AI narration. If you already have audio files, the base app is all you need.
Yes. Atlas is a universal binary and runs natively on both Apple Silicon (M1, M2, M3, M4) and Intel Macs. Conversion is notably faster on Apple Silicon due to hardware-accelerated audio encoding.
No. Your audio files and ebooks never leave your Mac during conversion. The only network usage is the TTS narration in AAC+ mode, which sends small chunks of text to Microsoft's Edge TTS service to generate speech — no audio is uploaded. The app requires no account and stores no personal data.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 2026

Atlas Audiobook Companion does not collect, store, transmit, or share any personal data. All audio processing and file conversion happens entirely on your Mac.

The app does not require an account, does not use analytics or crash reporting services, and does not contain advertising or tracking of any kind.

TTS Narration (AAC+ only): When using the ePub/PDF/MOBI import feature, small sections of text are sent to Microsoft's Edge TTS service to generate spoken audio. No audio files, ebook content, or identifying information is stored by this service. This is the only network request the app makes.

User Defaults: The app stores your preferences (such as default export path and preferred voice) locally on your Mac using macOS User Defaults. This data never leaves your device.

User content: Atlas Audiobook Companion processes audio and ebook files supplied by the user. The app does not verify the ownership, licensing status, or DRM status of imported files. Users are solely responsible for ensuring they have the legal right to convert any files they process with the app.

Payments: All purchases are processed by Apple. The developer does not receive or store any payment information.

For privacy questions, contact nidea.dev@gmail.com or open an issue at github.com/drgreenland/atlas-audiobook-companion.
